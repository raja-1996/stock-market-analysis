# Macro Economy Report — March 14 to March 21, 2026

**Analysis Period:** March 14–21, 2026 | **Report Compiled:** March 21, 2026

---

## Executive Summary

- **Stagflation risk rising**: February jobs report showed -92,000 payrolls (third decline in five months) while PPI surged +3.4% YoY — the Fed faces dual-mandate pressure from both sides simultaneously.
- **Fed holds at 3.50–3.75%** for the second consecutive meeting; dot plot signals only one cut in 2026; neutral rate revised up to 3.0%; Powell transition to Kevin Warsh looming in May.
- **Iran conflict is the dominant macro shock**: US military action starting ~Feb 28 pushed Brent crude above $101/bbl, crushed consumer sentiment to 2026 lows (UMich: 55.5), and forced global central banks into "wait-and-see" holds.
- **PPI pipeline warning**: Core PPI at +3.9% YoY (highest in over a year) signals inflation could re-accelerate in CPI/PCE over coming months — complicating the Fed's rate-cut path.
- **Jobless claims bright spot**: Weekly initial claims fell to 205K (below 215K forecast), confirming that layoffs remain low — labor market weakness is a hiring freeze, not mass layoffs.
- **Global central bank synchronization**: ECB, BoE, BoJ, and Fed all held rates on March 18–19; ECB raised its 2026 inflation forecast to 2.6% on oil price surge; BoE split 5–4.

## GDP & Growth Outlook

### ISM Manufacturing PMI (February 2026 — Released March 3, 2026)
Manufacturing PMI registered **52.4%** — down 0.2 points from January but above the 51.7% forecast. This marks the second straight month of manufacturing expansion and only the third expansion reading in 40 months. The New Orders Index came in at **55.8%** (still expansionary). Per ISM's historical correlation model, a 52.4% reading corresponds to approximately **+1.7% annualized real GDP growth** — modest but positive. The broader economy has remained in expansion for 16 consecutive months.

### Consumer Spending (January 2026 — BEA PCE Release, March 13, 2026)
Personal consumption expenditures rose **$81.1 billion (+0.4%)** in January, but **real PCE increased only $17.0 billion (+0.1%)** after inflation adjustment — notably soft. Q4 2025 GDP revisions delivered a downward revision to consumer spending, as previously reported services resilience was trimmed to align with weaker labor conditions and modest income growth.

### Global PMI Divergence
Preliminary Flash PMI data for February showed a diverging trend: stronger growth in Japan, UK, and Eurozone vs. slower US growth — a pattern persisting for several months. If sustained, this divergence may accelerate capital rotation away from US assets.

**Growth Signal Strength: 2 — Weak** | **Risk Rating: High**

### Analysis
The inflation picture deteriorated meaningfully this week. February PPI at **+0.7% MoM** — more than double the 0.3% forecast — signals pipeline inflationary pressure not yet captured in the CPI. Core PPI at **+3.9% YoY** is the highest reading in over a year. Key drivers: fresh/dry vegetables (+48.9%), diesel fuel (+13.9% for intermediate demand), chicken eggs, and gasoline — all heavily influenced by the Iran conflict oil shock.

The Fed's preferred PCE measure (January) came in at **+3.1% YoY for core** — meaningfully above the 2% target. The FOMC revised its 2026 year-end core PCE forecast up to **2.7%** at the March meeting, signaling it does not expect inflation to return to target this year.

UMich 1-year inflation expectations held flat at **3.4%** (ended six months of declines), and 5-year expectations at **3.2%** — both well above pre-pandemic norms. Anchoring risk is elevated.

**Inflation Signal Strength: 5 — Very Strong (Bearish)** | **Risk Rating: Very High**

## Fed & Interest Rates

- **Current rate**: 3.50%–3.75% (held for second consecutive meeting)
- **Next FOMC meeting**: April 28–29, 2026
- **Market expects next move**: At most **one cut in 2026** (some participants project no cuts)
- **Longer-run neutral rate**: Revised up to **3.0%** (from 2.9%)

### March 18–19 FOMC Decision
Rates held unanimously save one dissent — Governor **Stephen Miran** voted to cut 25bps; Governor **Christopher Waller** switched from his January cut-vote to a hold. The dot plot now shows 7 of 19 participants expecting no cuts in 2026 at all (up from 6 in December). The 2026 GDP forecast was raised to **2.4%** even as inflation forecasts were revised higher.

### Key Powell Quotes
> "Recent geopolitical developments are risks to both inflation and economic growth, but it is too early to take any policy action." — Chair Powell, March 19, 2026

Powell confirmed he will serve until the DOJ investigation is "well and truly over with transparency and finality." His term ends **May 2026**; Trump has nominated former Fed Governor **Kevin Warsh** as successor. Warsh is considered more hawkish, adding uncertainty to the rate-cut timeline.

### Analysis
The Fed is in a difficult position: it cannot cut rates with core PCE at 3.1% and PPI surging, but it cannot raise them with payrolls declining and unemployment at 4.4%. The "wait-and-see" stance is appropriate but offers no near-term relief for markets pricing in multiple cuts. The Warsh transition adds policy uncertainty as a new Fed Chair may signal a different reaction function.

**Fed Policy Signal Strength: 4 — Strong (Hawkish Hold)** | **Risk Rating: High**

| Wage growth (YoY) | +3.8% | +3.7% | ↑ Slightly rising |
| Labor force participation | 62.0% | 62.1% | ↓ Declining |

### Analysis
February's **-92,000 payrolls** shocked markets — the third decline in five months. The health care sector shed 28,000 jobs (including a -37,000 from a physician office strike). Federal government employment has now fallen **330,000 (-11%)** since the October 2024 peak, reflecting DOGE-era workforce reductions. Combined December–January revisions cut an additional 69,000 jobs, darkening the picture further.

The silver lining: weekly jobless claims fell to **205,000** — well below the 215,000 forecast — indicating layoffs remain low and the weakness is a **hiring freeze rather than mass terminations**. Wage growth at +3.8% YoY remains above inflation targets, adding to the Fed's inflation concerns.

The unemployment rate of 4.4% now approaches the **4.5% four-year high** from November 2024. Labor force participation declined 0.1 ppts to 62.0%, partially reflecting immigration-related labor supply reduction.

**Labor Market Signal Strength: 3 — Moderate (Mixed)** | **Risk Rating: High**

## Consumer & Business Sentiment

### University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment — Preliminary March 2026
- Headline: **55.5** (down from 56.6 in February; lowest of 2026)
- Current Economic Conditions: **57.8** (+1.2 ppts MoM; -6.0 ppts YoY)
- Consumer Expectations: **54.1** (-2.5 ppts MoM)
- 1-year inflation expectations: **3.4%** (unchanged — ended 6 months of declines)
- 5-year inflation expectations: **3.2%** (down from 3.3%)

The Iran conflict drove the decline: surveys before the military action showed improvement, but post-conflict interviews erased all gains. Personal finance expectations fell **7.5% nationally** across income, age, and political cohorts — a rare broad-based decline. Final March reading due March 27.

### Conference Board Consumer Confidence — February 2026
- Headline: **91.2** (+2.2 ppts from revised 89.0 in January)
- Present Situation: **120.0** (-1.8 ppts)
- Expectations: **72.0** (+4.8 ppts)
- Note: Expectations below **80** historically signals elevated recession risk — current reading of 72.0 is a warning sign.

### NFIB Small Business Optimism — February 2026
- Index: **98.8** (down from 99.3; below 99.7 forecast; second consecutive monthly decline)
- Net % expecting higher real sales: down 8 points to +8%
- Remained above historical average for 10th straight month
- Uncertainty index declined (a positive signal within the soft headline)

### Summary
Consumer and business sentiment is under pressure from geopolitical shock, high inflation expectations, and deteriorating labor conditions. The Conference Board Expectations Index at 72 is in recession-warning territory. Small business confidence is softening. Only the unemployment expectations component of the Conference Board survey showed meaningful improvement.

**Sentiment Signal Strength: 4 — Strong (Bearish)** | **Risk Rating: High**

## Market Implications

**Bullish factors:**
- Manufacturing PMI at 52.4% — second straight month of expansion; economy has been growing 16 consecutive months
- Initial jobless claims at 205K — layoffs remain contained; labor market weakness is a hiring freeze
- Dollar strength benefits US consumers on imported goods (partial offset to energy inflation)
- Gold near record highs ($5,021) — risk-off assets performing; potential store-of-value demand

**Bearish factors:**
- Stagflationary combination: declining payrolls + rising PPI pipeline inflation
- Conference Board Expectations at 72 — below the 80-threshold that historically precedes recessions
- PPI surging at +3.4% YoY (highest in a year); core PPI +3.9% YoY — inflation re-acceleration risk
- Fed on hold with no near-term cut catalyst; Warsh transition adds policy uncertainty
- Iran conflict + oil above $101/bbl — energy shock feeding into all inflation metrics
- Consumer sentiment at 2026 lows; personal finance expectations down 7.5% nationally
- Global PMI divergence: non-US growth accelerating as US slows — capital rotation risk

**Key risks to watch:**
- **April 9 PCE release (February data)**: Will confirm whether the PPI surge has flowed through to consumer inflation
- **March 31 Conference Board** Consumer Confidence: Will Iran conflict further depress expectations below 70?
- **April 28–29 FOMC**: First meeting potentially under new Warsh leadership — market will parse every word for policy shift
- **Tariff escalation**: EU–US trade negotiations outcome could be a major macro catalyst in either direction
- **Iran conflict duration**: Sustained conflict keeps oil elevated and threatens both growth and inflation simultaneously
