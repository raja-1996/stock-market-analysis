**Signal Strength: 3 (Moderate)** — US growth is positive but decelerating; Iran war is a new headwind.

## 3. Federal Reserve & Interest Rates

- **Current rate**: **3.5%–3.75%** (held unanimously 11-1 at March 18, 2026 FOMC)
- **Dot plot signal**: **1 rate cut in 2026**, 1 in 2027 — but timing "uncertain"
- **Market expectation**: June cut probability collapsed to ~35%; market now pricing ~1 cut in 2026

### Key Quotes
> *"Monetary policy is not on a preset course and we'll make our decisions on a meeting by meeting basis."* — Chair Powell, March 18

> *"The current rate range is within a range of neutral, and it is too soon to tell what the effect of the conflict in the Middle East will be on the economy."* — Chair Powell

### Analysis
The Fed is in classic **wait-and-see mode**, caught between sticky inflation (2.8% CPI, 3.1% core) and softening growth (-92K payrolls in February). The Iran war has introduced a **stagflationary shock** — oil above $80/bbl threatens to push headline inflation higher while simultaneously threatening growth. This is the most challenging macro backdrop since 2022. The Fed's hands are effectively tied: cutting would risk inflation re-acceleration; hiking would crush a weakening labor market.

**Quantitative tightening**: No update this week; QT is ongoing at reduced pace.

| NFIB Small Business Optimism (Feb) | **98.8** | -0.5 pts | 45th percentile; 10th straight month above average |

### Analysis
Consumer sentiment is at historically **depressed levels** (2nd percentile) — comparable to or below readings that preceded all six recessions since 1978. The Iran war and higher gasoline prices have triggered a sharp deterioration in **expectations**, even as current conditions remain somewhat stable.

NFIB small businesses show a mixed picture: headline optimism dipped but uncertainty lessened. Labor quality as "top problem" fell to its lowest since April 2020 — a positive for businesses but signals labor supply tightening.

The divergence between current conditions (rising) and expectations (falling) is a **classic late-cycle pattern** — consumers feel okay now but are anxious about what's coming.

### Geopolitical Risk: US–Iran War
The US and Israeli strikes on Iran (late February 2026) triggered a **Strait of Hormuz crisis** — the single biggest macro event of 2026 so far. Key impacts:
- Oil prices surged 9–13% (Brent above $80/bbl)
- Goldman Sachs targets $110/bbl; JPMorgan sees $120–130 if disruptions persist
- Europe's energy supply is threatened — ECB revised inflation forecast upward
- All major central banks paused rate actions to assess economic fallout

### Trade & Tariffs
- US tariff policy continues to suppress export demand — S&P Global PMI showed exports declined for the **8th consecutive month**.
- Tariff pass-through is now materializing in manufacturing prices — input costs rising at fastest pace since August.
